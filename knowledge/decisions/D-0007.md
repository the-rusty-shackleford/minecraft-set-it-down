# D-0007 — Copies never pass through each other

**The ask.** Rusty, passing on the friend's feedback (2026-10-07): "should also be conscious of
stackable items clipping with 3d models in effect".

**Why.**

- A cluster's table spreads its copies for flat sprites, all at one height. Overlapping sprites
  only overlap; overlapping 3D models pass through each other. Four 3D apples at the table's
  spacing sink well into one another.
- A pile climbs by the thickness of what is drawn. Eight of Fresh Food's 3D loaves, three
  sixteenths thick, would stand three quarters of a block out of a box a quarter deep.

**Decision.**

- **Flat copies lie on one another.** A flat copy is no thicker than a quarter of its length. If
  flat copies would overlap, each lies a layer above the one before, at the table's places and
  size, as steaks or lumps of coal set down do. A sprite's cluster looks as in 1.0.0.
- **Thick copies are spread apart.** Copies on one layer are spread apart, as a multiple of the
  table's offsets, until no two overlap. They are measured by what each shows (a 3D model's whole
  box) and turned by their slots, tested by separating axes. They are then shrunk together until
  all they show lies within the display's square.
- **Heaped, not towered.** A pile whose layers would stand taller than its box (an item's box, a
  quarter block untipped) is laid out as a cluster is: a square, then a second layer.
- **No taller than its box.** Several copies never stand taller than their box; they are shrunk
  together if they would.
- **A single item keeps its own thickness.** A block is a half-block cube, as D-0006 says, with a
  box to match.
- A table that needed none of this keeps its own sizes and places, so a sprite's pile is as in
  1.0.0.

**Alternatives rejected.**

- *Spreading flat copies too.* Two 3D steaks, spread side by side and shrunk into their square,
  came out half their size (the booth's photo); lying on each other they keep it.
- *Shrinking a tall pile into its box.* Eight loaves would each be a third of their size; heaped,
  they keep nearly half (0.46), standing a third of the box's depth.
- *Spreading by a sprite's whole square.* Every cluster of coal or seeds would spread wide for
  pixels that are not there.
- *Letting a heap reach past its square.* The square is what the edge clamp keeps on the face, and
  what is clicked.

**Verification.**

- JUnit:
  - Clusters of two to eight 3D apples: no two copies on a layer overlap, and every copy stays
    within the square.
  - Clusters of two to eight 3D steaks: no two pass through each other. Two keep the table's
    places and size, overlapping, the second a layer out.
  - Heaps of two to eight thick lumps stand no taller than the box; five stand exactly as tall.
  - Eight loaves heap; two loaves, and eight sprites, pile.
  - Each rule's mutation is caught (`devtools/verification/mutations-1.1.0.py`).
- The booth photographs eight loaves, eight cookies and four carrots (all 3D), and the old table
  beside 1.0.0's: its apples, steaks and coal.
