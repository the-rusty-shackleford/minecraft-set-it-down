# D-0010 — A fish lies on its side

**What was seen.** Heft's review photos for nfx, in a copy of Rusty's instance (2026-10-26): a set-down cod, drawn
by Fresh Food's 3D model through Modefite, stood upright on its belly, turned corner to corner, its fins through
the bench. Rusty, on what Heft should take over: "anything that doesn't look like what it's supposed to".

**Why.** D-0005 stands a held model on its base when it is no taller than twice its narrowest width. Fresh Food's
fish are built swimming, along z, thin side to side: a cod 6 x 6.12 x 15, a salmon 5 x 8 x 25. Each passes, so it
stood on a keel its own width, and then, long across the face, was laid corner to corner.

**Decision.** A base at least twice as long as it is wide is a keel; a thing on one at least three quarters as tall
as the keel is wide rolls off it onto its side (`Rest.rolls`). Measured against Fresh Food's models: the cod,
cooked cod and salmon roll; the loaf (6 x 3 x 11, half as tall as wide), the potato (9 is under twice 5), the
steak, the apple and every round thing stand as before.

**Alternatives rejected.** *A lower stand ratio overall* (one and a half): the apple, 7.43 on 5, would fall, which
D-0005 rejected (it read as a red block on its side). *Naming fish*: Set It Down measures, it does not know items.
